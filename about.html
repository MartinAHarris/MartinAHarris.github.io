About Me

I earned my bachelor's degree from the University of Oregon in 2017, where I first became fascinated by volcanoes and the dynamic landscapes they create. I continued my training at the University of British Columbia, completing a master's degree in 2021 focused on glaciovolcanic landforms and detailed field mapping around the Mount Meager Volcanic Complex. These experiences strengthened my interest in volcanic processes and the ways in which field observations can be integrated with laboratory and analytical approaches.

I completed my PhD in 2025 at the University at Buffalo, where I shifted my focus to the physical properties of basaltic lavas. My dissertation research centered on developing and engineering scientific instruments capable of collecting in‑situ viscosity measurements during active eruptions. I also conducted high‑temperature remelting experiments to quantify lava rheology under controlled laboratory conditions, allowing direct comparison between natural and experimental data.

I currently serve as a Postdoctoral Research Associate at Ruhr University Bochum, where my research investigates the physical properties of submarine basalts. My work examines how basaltic lava rheology evolves during submarine eruptions, with particular emphasis on phase proportions, temperature, and redox conditions. This research aims to improve our understanding of submarine volcanic processes and the ways in which lava flow behavior differs between subaerial and submarine environments.

Broadly, my research interests span physical volcanology, lava rheology, geologic mapping, and the integration of field, laboratory, and geochemical approaches to understand volcanic systems. I am committed to advancing collaborative, instrument‑driven studies of active eruptions and applying new insights to improve hazard assessments and modeling strategies for future volcanic activity.
